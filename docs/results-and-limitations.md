# Scientific Results, Analysis, and Critical Limitations

The table below presents the verified model outputs obtained from running the workflow at 77 K and 4 K for an 8 Gb, 16-bank DRAM array at the 32 nm technology node ($V_{DD} = 1.2\text{ V}, V_{th0} = 0.4\text{ V}$):

## 2. Key Scientific Findings

1. **Dramatic Leakage Suppression**: Standby leakage power drops by **80.96%** going from 77 K down to 4 K (and by several orders of magnitude compared to 300 K). This is the single most compelling advantage of cryogenic memory operation: refresh cycles can be postponed by orders of magnitude without losing charge.
2. **Timing Is Mixed**: While $t_{RCD}$ improves by 0.77%, other latencies ($t_{RAS}, t_{RC}, t_{CAS}$) exhibit modest increases (1% to 3%). This reflects the architectural trade-offs made by CACTI when sizing buffers and interconnect lines under cryogenic driver characteristics.
3. **Energy Scaling**: Dynamic energy ($C \cdot V^2$) remains relatively flat between 77 K and 4 K (showing a slight +1.8% difference) because capacitive charging is primarily determined by physical geometry and supply voltage ($V_{DD} = 1.2\text{ V}$), which does not scale with temperature alone.

## 3. Critical Limitations & Scientific Caveats

To ensure research integrity, teammates and readers must be aware of the following four critical caveats:

### Caveat 1: Model-Generated Output vs. Physical DIMM Measurements
All reported numerical data are generated by software models (CryoModel, CACTI, ngspice). They are **not experimental measurements from a physical DIMM inside a dilution refrigerator**.

### Caveat 2: CACTI Internal Temperature Handling
CryoMEM injects cryogenic device parameters ($I_{on}, I_{off}, I_{gate}, V_{th}, R_{wire}$) into CACTI via Protocol Buffers. However, **CACTI's internal temperature configuration remains fixed at 300 K** in this code path. Thus, thermal resistance and junction temperature dynamics are not modeled end-to-end.

### Caveat 3: Supply Voltage Confounding in MOSFET Generator
In the CryoMOSFET generator:
- The **reference run** uses $V_{DD} = 0.9\text{ V}$.
- The **cryogenic target runs** use $V_{DD} = 1.2\text{ V}$.
Because supply voltage differs between reference and target, drive-current gains cannot be attributed strictly to temperature reduction alone.

### Caveat 4: SPICE Simulations Use Generic Room-Temperature Model
The 1T and 3T DRAM circuit netlists (`dram_1t.sp` and `dram_3t.sp`) utilize a generic Level-1 MOSFET model (`nmos_generic.inc`) at 27 °C (300 K). They serve to demonstrate bitcell circuit mechanics, write-$V_{th}$ loss, and non-destructive read behavior, and **must not be cited as empirical cryogenic silicon data**.
